how the distances work

Visible distance compares the cells each viewpoint can see after washers, dryers and furniture block the rays.

Attention distance measures the effort needed to move one attention pattern onto another across the room.

The attention patterns are written for this simulation. They are not eye tracking.

Proximity rules use body distance, machine clearance, exit clearance and shared attention. The plan uses an approximate scale of 1.8 centimeters per drawing unit.

Position recognizes between, adjacent and among. Movement uses start and end distances for toward and away, and the whole recorded path for past. Click an open tile to walk around furniture.

The room is a stylized cutaway. Sight is computed on its ground plane with solid furniture footprints. It does not model over-the-table vision or human cognition.

Eight customers seek a washer, a dryer and a folding table. The attendant inspects machines and repairs faults. Each person has an individual map, a limited forward field of view, remembered landmarks, and a goal. Plans use their private maps, not the full scene.

The notebook shows personal knowledge. Its dark tiles are unknown, pale tiles are remembered, and bright tiles are visible now. Remembered machine states can become out of date. Click a remembered place to send that person there, or let them decide.

Targets are 240 simulation seconds per customer and 180 seconds for the attendant to inspect all 14 machines. Distance walked, time, coverage and repairs are measured. Machine cycles are deliberately compressed for the workshop; these are illustrative rules, not measured human behavior.

Add nine more people to compare a busy room with a quiet one. Shared traces show how many different people passed through each tile, plus counts at landmarks that several people discovered. This observer overlay is never passed into an individual's memory or planner.
